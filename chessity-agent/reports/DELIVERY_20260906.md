## Completed results at 120 seconds plus 0.5 seconds

In their direct 12-game comparison, v1.14 scored **5 wins, 4 draws and 3 losses** against v1.23. This supports keeping v1.14; the small sample does not establish an absolute strongest-possible engine.

| Build | Opponent's Stockfish setting | Wins | Draws | Losses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| v1.23 | 2200 | 0 | 2 | 6 |
| Selected v1.14 | 2000 | 2 | 1 | 9 |

The highest individual winning setting was **2200 for v1.14**, and **2000 for v1.23**. Neither result is a measured human/site Elo. Conditional on the nominal Stockfish settings serving as rating anchors, the descriptive estimates are approximately 1874 for v1.14 (broad band 1600–2200) and 1788 for v1.23 (1500–2100). Hardware, calibration and small-sample uncertainty remain.

All 52 new games passed move, clock, increment, result and frozen-source audits with no runtime failures. The earlier 54-game selection session was also audited. Training improved a few raw-policy answers, but fixed-node choices were unchanged and practical tests did not establish an overall improvement. A post-test sample contained 27 resolved assessments, including two verified major errors, and 21 unresolved positions; it is not a complete mistake count.

The Carlsen, puzzle and supplied Hikaru/Gotham pilot work is complete. The newest data pilot verified 2,681 positions, including 388 opening decisions, and trained the compatible policy with broad-data replay. The original 300k run means 300,000 positions, not games. Existing data and unsuccessful checkpoints remain locally available. The public repository contains agent versions, own development code and test evidence; downloaded player archives and external engine executables remain outside the public export.

Benchmark games use fixed weights. Wins and losses affect selection and descriptive estimates; they do not update the neural network. Future reinforcement learning requires separate training games and fresh evaluation.
