# Bounded elite-case learning experiment

This follows the completed three-phase search assessment. The selected phase build is preserved as the baseline, and no old runtime or completed evaluation record is edited.

The supplied `Elite-Blitz-Findings-and-Cases.md` contains 22 case entries, representing 21 distinct legal positions from 20 source games. Its referenced 1,862-game corpus and positive-imitation files have not been located. The user has been asked for the companion pack's path; this configuration explicitly uses the supplied cases. The source Markdown SHA-256 is recorded in `configs/elite-case-pilot.json`. Reading the prose alone does not update network weights.

Each case is independently checked at 80k and 320k Stockfish nodes with full legal root coverage. Only completed common-depth unbounded UCI score snapshots are used. Unstable estimates, unresolved non-exact mate objectives and inadequate draw-rule contexts are quarantined. The preceding histories missing from source-case FENs cannot be reconstructed from the Markdown. Exact short mate proofs are distinguished from finite engine estimates.

The existing 935–64–32–1 policy receives verified board/move supervision, broad train-only Carlsen/Witty replay and existing verified phase/puzzle replay. Whole known validation source games and exact/mirrored validation boards are excluded. All public cases are training material. Previously used validation sets choose checkpoints; no new unseen elite-corpus holdout is claimed.

The budget is four initial epochs, followed by two rounds of four full training games: White and Black against each of the 2400 and 2600 nominal Stockfish settings. Each game uses 120 seconds plus 0.5 seconds per move. Every recorded ply is reviewed after the game, with full history reconstructed from the actual game start. Verified own moves and corrections enter a two-epoch update before the next training game. Opponent moves are analysed as context and are not rewarded as the learner's actions.

A supervised outcome bonus mixes up to 10% probability into a verified sound action from a win, or 5% from a draw; unsound actions get no outcome bonus even when their game was won. Losing games still train corrected teacher alternatives. This is outcome-guided supervised learning, not a policy-gradient RL algorithm or a position-value label based on a terminal result. A matched teacher-only branch receives identical training examples and update counts without that bonus. Examples and recorded-game exposure are capped. Epoch zero remains eligible to avoid retaining a validation regression.

After eight adaptation games, weights are frozen. The final schedule contains 16 games against the preserved best, eight against the matched teacher-only branch, and 16 each against nominal 2400 and 2600 Stockfish settings. The eight evaluation opening families are held out from this adaptation schedule and checked for finite balance before any evaluation game. They may occur in older source histories. Colour-pair score intervals, outright wins, draw/loss counts and reliability failures are reported separately. Adaptive training games do not establish playing strength.

The practical consistency criterion is a lower 95% Wilson bound above 50% on outright wins at each tested setting. Its assumptions and the accompanying paired score intervals are reported; meeting it would be local pilot evidence, not a human/site Elo certificate. Promotion requires the paired lower 95% score bound above 50% against the preserved best, at least 50% score against the teacher-only branch and no candidate runtime failure in fresh evaluation. Otherwise the earlier best remains the download.

The pilot stops after the declared two rounds and fixed evaluation, whether or not a 2600 win occurs. This avoids an indefinite compute/usage commitment for a strength target that this small architecture may not reach. Every candidate, actual checkpoint selection, per-game review, reward exposure, final ZIP and promotion decision is retained. Only the best verified competition ZIP is recommended, with experiments labelled separately on GitHub.
